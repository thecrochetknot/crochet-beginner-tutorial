How to Crochet for Absolute Beginners

A simple crochet tutorial for beginners. Tools, stitches and your first project, step by step.

Do you want to learn crochet, but you do not know where to start? This crochet tutorial for beginners explains every step in plain words. You do not need any skills. You only need a few simple tools and a little patience.

You will learn:

  • The tools every beginner needs
  • How to hold your hook and yarn
  • How to make your first stitches, step by step
  • Easy projects to try first

What You Need to Start Crocheting

Easy crochet for beginners needs very few supplies. One trip to a craft store is enough.

Yarn

  • Choose medium weight yarn (worsted weight, category 4).
  • Pick a smooth, light color like cream or light blue. You can see each stitch clearly.
  • Avoid fuzzy or sparkly yarn. It hides your stitches.
  • Cotton and acrylic are soft, cheap, and easy to find.

Crochet Hook

  • Start with a 5 mm (size H-8) hook. It matches medium weight yarn.
  • Choose a hook with a comfortable handle, so your hand does not get tired.
  • Aluminum hooks glide smoothly. Wooden hooks grip the yarn more.

Scissors and Yarn Needle

  • Small, sharp scissors cut yarn cleanly.
  • A yarn needle has a big eye and a blunt tip. Use it to weave in loose yarn ends.

Stitch Marker

A stitch marker is a small clip or ring that marks your place.

No stitch marker? Use a safety pin, a hair pin, or a paper clip instead.

Crochet Terms Every Beginner Should Know

Crochet instructions use short words. Learn these first and every pattern becomes easier.

Common Words

StitchOne loop worked into the fabric.
Chain (ch)The first row of loops. Every project starts here.
Slip knotThe first loop on your hook.
RowOne line of stitches from end to end.
TurnFlip your work to start the next row.
Yarn over (yo)Wrap the yarn over your hook.

Common Short Forms

  • sc: single crochet
  • sl st: slip stitch
  • st / sts: stitch / stitches

How to Hold a Crochet Hook

There are two simple ways. Choose the one that feels natural.

Pencil Grip

  • Hold the hook like a pencil.
  • Rest it between your thumb and first finger.

Knife Grip

  • Hold the hook like a knife.
  • Your palm sits over the handle.
Keep your hand relaxed. A tight grip causes sore fingers.

How to Hold the Yarn

Good yarn tension makes your stitches even. This step needs practice, so be patient.

Step-by-Step Yarn Hold

  1. Hold your hook in your right hand.
  2. Weave the yarn through the fingers of your left hand.
  3. Wrap it around your pinky, then over your ring and middle fingers.
  4. Rest it under your index finger.
  5. Use your thumb and middle finger to hold your work steady.

Left-handed? Do everything in reverse.

How to Make a Slip Knot

Every project begins with a slip knot. This is the first of the crochet steps for beginners.

Step-by-Step Slip Knot

  1. Make a loop, leaving a 6-inch tail.
  2. Pull the working yarn through the loop to make a second loop.
  3. Put your hook in the new loop.
  4. Pull the tail gently to tighten. The loop should slide easily on the hook.

How to Chain Stitch (ch)

The chain stitch is the base of almost every pattern.

Step-by-Step Chain Stitch

  1. Hold the slip knot on your hook.
  2. Yarn over (wrap the yarn over the hook).
  3. Pull the yarn through the loop on your hook.
  4. You have made one chain.
  5. Repeat until you have 15 chains.
Do not pull too tight. Loose chains are easier to work into.

How to Read Your Chain

Look closely at your chain. You will see three parts:

  • The V shapes on the front
  • The bumps on the back
  • The loop on your hook (do not count this one)

For your first row, work into the center of each V. This keeps your edge neat.

How to Single Crochet (sc)

Single crochet is the most basic stitch. It is the best stitch for learning crochet.

Step-by-Step Single Crochet

  1. Skip the first chain next to your hook.
  2. Put your hook into the second chain from the hook.
  3. Yarn over and pull up a loop. You now have 2 loops on the hook.
  4. Yarn over again.
  5. Pull through both loops.
  6. You have made one single crochet.
  7. Repeat in each chain across.

Your First Row of Single Crochet

Work one single crochet in each chain. When you reach the end:

  1. Chain 1.
  2. Turn your work.
  3. Work one single crochet into each stitch across.

Repeat for as many rows as you like. Congratulations, you are crocheting!

How to Count Stitches and Rows

Counting keeps your edges straight. Many beginners skip this and end up with a wavy shape.

Counting Stitches

  • Look at the top of each row. Count the V shapes.
  • Do not count the turning chain.
  • Use a stitch marker in the first stitch of each row.

Counting Rows

  • Count the sideways bumps along the edge.
  • Each bump is one row.

Common Beginner Mistakes (and Easy Fixes)

Every beginner makes these. Do not worry.

Your work gets wider or narrower. You added or skipped stitches. Count after every row.
Stitches feel too tight. Relax your hand. Try a hook one size bigger.
Stitches look too loose. Hold the yarn a little firmer.
You cannot find the first stitch. Use a stitch marker.
A mistake appears. Pull out the hook gently, then undo the stitches until you reach the error. This is called "frogging."

Make Your First Swatch

A swatch is a small practice square. It builds your confidence before a real project.

Swatch Steps

  1. Chain 15.
  2. Work single crochet in each chain.
  3. Make 15 rows.
  4. Check your edges. They should be straight.

Practice a few swatches until your stitches look even. Learning to crochet takes time, so enjoy the process.

How to Finish Your Project

Finishing makes your work look neat and keeps it from coming apart.

Fasten Off

  1. Cut the yarn, leaving a 6-inch tail.
  2. Pull the tail through the last loop.
  3. Tighten gently.

Weave in the Ends

  1. Thread the tail onto your yarn needle.
  2. Weave it through the stitches for about 2 inches.
  3. Cut the extra yarn.

What to Learn After the Basics

Ready for more? Learn these next:

  • Half double crochet (hdc): taller than single crochet.
  • Double crochet (dc): a fast stitch for blankets and bags.
  • Slip stitch: great for joining and finishing.
  • Crochet in the round: makes hats, bags, and toys.
  • Reading patterns: learn to follow written instructions and charts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is crochet hard to learn?

No. Crochet is easy to learn with simple stitches and a little practice. Most people finish a small project in a few days.

What is the easiest stitch for beginners?

Single crochet. It is simple, steady, and used in many patterns.

How long does it take to learn crochet?

Most beginners learn the basic stitches in a few hours. Smooth, even stitches take a few weeks of practice.

Which yarn is best for beginners?

Smooth, light-colored, medium weight yarn in cotton or acrylic.

Can I learn crochet online for free?

Yes. Free video lessons and written tutorials can help you learn crochet step by step. Use this crochet tutorial for beginners as your starting point.

Is crochet or knitting easier?

Most people find crochet easier. You use one hook and only one live loop at a time.

Final Thoughts

You now know the tools, the terms, and the stitches. Start with a simple swatch, keep your tension relaxed, and practice a little every day. This crochet tutorial for beginners is only the start. Pick up your hook and begin!
